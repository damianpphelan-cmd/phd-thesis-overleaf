\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis asks whether the organisational culture of a school---the relational
and behavioural environment its pupils experience day to day---can be measured
credibly at scale, and whether it predicts how much pupils learn.

The first paper constructs the measures. For \NTierOne{} English secondary
schools a research visit and a headteacher interview yield a gold standard in
two parts, an enacted score from what an observer recorded and an espoused
score from what the head reported, for each of two dimensions: warmth, the
relational quality of the school, and strictness, its behavioural structure.
Inspection reports, behaviour policies and school websites are scored for the
national population of around 3,330 schools by language-model instruments, and
prediction models fitted where both exist extend the gold standard to the rest.
Strictness is legible in public text and warmth is not: inspection-report
strictness is correlated with observed strictness at $r = \CorrOfstedStrictness$
and a text-only model predicts it out of sample at $\RidgeBStrictness$, while
no text source is correlated with observed warmth. Espoused and enacted
culture diverge, and more for warmth: headteachers rate their schools
\GapWarmth{} points warmer on a ten-point scale than observers record and
\GapStrictness{} points stricter, and every public source tracks the espoused
warmth measure rather than the enacted one.

The second paper estimates the association between observed culture and pupil
progress. Across the visited schools, a standard deviation of observed warmth
is associated with $\hat{\beta}_W = \PrimarySpecW$ of a grade higher
Progress~8 and a standard deviation of observed strictness with
$\hat{\beta}_S = \PrimarySpecS$ ($n = \PrimarySpecN$), conditioning on prior
attainment, intake composition, school type and the pre-pandemic inspection
grade; both are significant at the one per cent level, and the schools that
combine high warmth with high strictness outperform the rest by a third of a
grade. Observed teaching quality does not account for the association.
Substituting the headteacher's own account for the observed scores removes
the warmth association, and the strictness score read from inspection reports
reproduces the strictness result across \NNationalStrictness{} schools with
the inspection grade held constant.

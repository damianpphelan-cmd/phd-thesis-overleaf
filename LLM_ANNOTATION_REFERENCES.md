# Verified references for the rubric-guided-AI precedent passage (Ch2 §4.1)

Every entry below was verified against the publisher's page or the archive on
24 Aug 2026 (not cited from model memory). One-line relevance notes follow
each. BibTeX to be generated from these at write-up time.

## Core precedent (economics)

- Eloundou, T., S. Manning, P. Mishkin and D. Rock (2024). "GPTs are GPTs:
  Labor market impact potential of LLMs." *Science* 384(6702): 1306-1308.
  doi:10.1126/science.adj0998. Working paper: arXiv:2303.10130.
  — A written rubric applied by both human raters and GPT-4, with agreement
  reported: the canonical rubric-guided-AI measurement design.
- Hansen, A. L. and S. Kazinnik (2023). "Can ChatGPT Decipher Fedspeak?"
  SSRN 4399406; presented at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York FinTech
  conference. — GPT classifying FOMC policy stance from written
  instructions, benchmarked against human expert coders.
- Ludwig, J., S. Mullainathan and A. Rambachan (2025). "Large Language
  Models: An Applied Econometric Framework." NBER Working Paper 33344;
  arXiv:2412.07031. — The conditions under which LLM-produced labels are
  valid in empirical economics; validation against ground truth is the
  central requirement. The thesis's grid directly answers its
  measurement-vs-statistical-alternative question.
- CBESS/UEA working paper 24-01. "Using Large Language Models for Text
  Classification in Experimental Economics."
  (ueaeco.github.io/working-papers/papers/cbess/UEA-CBESS-24-01.pdf)
  — GPT-4 vs human annotators on experiment transcripts; instruction
  wording varied explicitly.

## Adjacent social science

- Gilardi, F., M. Alizadeh and M. Kubli (2023). "ChatGPT outperforms
  crowd-workers for text-annotation tasks." *PNAS* 120(30): e2305016120.
  doi:10.1073/pnas.2305016120. — The most-cited LLM-as-annotator result.
- Tornberg, P. (2025). "Large Language Models Outperform Expert Coders and
  Supervised Classifiers at Annotating Political Social Media Messages."
  *Social Science Computer Review*. doi:10.1177/08944393241286471.
- Wang, R. and D. Demszky (2023). "Is ChatGPT a Good Teacher Coach?
  Measuring Zero-Shot Performance for Scoring and Providing Actionable
  Insights on Classroom Instruction." *Proceedings of the 18th Workshop on
  Innovative Use of NLP for Building Educational Applications (BEA)*,
  ACL Anthology 2023.bea-1.53. — Classroom transcripts scored against the
  MQI observation instrument; CAUTIONARY: zero-shot agreement with human
  raters was weak and outputs unstable — cite as precedent for the design,
  not as evidence it always works.

## The pre-LLM lineage (rubric-coded text in economics)

- Baker, S. R., N. Bloom and S. J. Davis (2016). "Measuring Economic Policy
  Uncertainty." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 131(4): 1593-1636.
  doi:10.1093/qje/qjw024. — Newspaper-based index validated by ~12,000
  human readings following a written coding guide: the LLM enters an old
  workflow in the coder's seat.
- Bandiera, O., A. Prat, S. Hansen and R. Sadun (2020). "CEO Behavior and
  Firm Performance." *Journal of Political Economy* 128(4): 1325-1369.
  doi:10.1086/705331. — CEO time-use diaries coded into behavioural types
  (survey + machine-learning step); NOTE the coding is ML-clustering of
  diary data, not a pure hand rubric — describe accurately if cited.

## Positioning sentence (for the draft)

Most of this literature validates LLM labels against human labels of the
same text; the thesis's grid validates against an independent behavioural
criterion (trained observers' visit scores), and runs the
rubric-vs-learned-weights comparison that Ludwig-Mullainathan-Rambachan
call for. Cite the best-practice guides (arXiv:2402.05129; arXiv:2503.22040)
for the pin-freeze-validate checklist the design already satisfies.

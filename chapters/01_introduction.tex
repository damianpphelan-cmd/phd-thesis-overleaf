\section{Overview of the Thesis}
\label{sec:intro_overview}

The remainder of this thesis is organised as follows.
\Cref{ch:paper1} develops the measurement framework. It constructs gold-standard
warmth and strictness scores for 102 schools from structured researcher visits
and headteacher interviews, and then asks how far those scores can be recovered
from text that exists for every school---Ofsted reports, behaviour policies,
school websites---using large language models calibrated against the
gold-standard sample. The central finding is an asymmetry: strictness is
recoverable from public text and warmth is not, in any source.

\Cref{ch:paper2} uses those scores as explanatory variables. It estimates the
association between school culture and pupil progress between Key Stage~2 and
Key Stage~4, and shows that warmth and strictness predict Progress~8 with
near-equal magnitude, that observed teaching quality does not displace either,
and that substituting headteacher self-reports for observed scores collapses the
warmth coefficient---the measurement asymmetry of \cref{ch:paper1} reappearing
as a predictive one.

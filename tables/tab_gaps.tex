Scores are z-standardised within instrument before the gap is formed. In the gold tier
neither gap adds anything beyond the enacted level, so the coherence hypothesis closes
there. Nationally, the absolute strictness gap is positive for the behaviour-policy
source ($p=0.035$) and same-signed for the website source ($p=0.24$), but both die once
component quadratics enter; the surviving curvature sits on the squared enacted
(Ofsted) score for both strictness sources ($\beta \approx +0.015$, $p<0.01$), while the
squared document score is never significant. The absolute-gap effect is therefore
extremity in the components, not dissonance between them. The signed BP warmth gap is
the known negative BP-warmth marginal reported in Chapter 2, appearing here in gap form.
Effects are tiny (0.01--0.02 of a grade per SD) and should be read in that light.

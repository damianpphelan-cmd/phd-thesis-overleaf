\footnotesize\textit{Notes:} Each row regresses average Progress 8 on the enacted (observed) score, the espoused-minus-enacted gap, and the primary control set. The first column enters the signed gap, the second the absolute gap, and the third re-enters the absolute gap alongside quadratics in both component scores --- the robustness check that decides whether an absolute-gap effect is genuine dissonance or just curvature in the components. Standard errors in parentheses (HC3; behaviour-policy rows

\sym{*} \(p<0.10\), \sym{**} \(p<0.05\), \sym{***} \(p<0.01\).
Scores are z-standardised within instrument and estimation sample before the gap is
formed; late-entry schools excluded. In the visited sample neither gap adds anything beyond
the enacted level. Nationally, the absolute strictness gap is positive for the
behaviour-policy source ($p=0.00$) and same-signed for the website
source ($p=0.01$); once component quadratics enter, the squared enacted (Ofsted)
score carries $\beta = +0.026$ ($p=0.000$) for the behaviour-policy pair and
$+0.027$ ($p=0.000$) for the website pair, while the squared document score is
$+0.004$ ($p=0.44$) and $-0.004$ ($p=0.52$). Effects are tiny
(0.01--0.02 of a grade per SD) and should be read in that light.
